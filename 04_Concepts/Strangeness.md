# Strangeness

## Core Idea

Strangeness $S$ is a quantum number assigned to particles containing strange quarks. It is conserved in strong and electromagnetic interactions but can change by $0$ or $\pm 1$ in weak interactions. Its existence explains why strange particles are produced quickly (strong) yet decay slowly (weak).

## Meaning

Assignment rule:

- A strange quark $s$ has $S = -1$.
- An anti-strange quark $\bar{s}$ has $S = +1$.
- All other quarks (and leptons, photons) have $S = 0$.
- Multi-quark particles inherit $S$ as the sum over their quarks.

Examples:

| Particle | Quark content | $S$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $K^{+}$ | $u\bar{s}$ | $+1$ |
| $K^{-}$ | $\bar{u}s$ | $-1$ |
| $K^{0}$ | $d\bar{s}$ | $+1$ |
| $\Lambda^{0}$ (lambda baryon) | $uds$ | $-1$ |
| $\pi^{+}$ | $u\bar{d}$ | $0$ |
| proton, neutron | $uud$, $udd$ | $0$ |

**Conservation rule (AQA):**

- **Strong interaction:** $\Delta S = 0$.
- **Electromagnetic interaction:** $\Delta S = 0$.
- **Weak interaction:** $\Delta S = 0, \pm 1$.

Because strangeness is conserved in the strong force, strange particles are produced **in pairs** of opposite strangeness (associated production): e.g. $\pi^{-} + p \rightarrow K^{0} + \Lambda^{0}$, where $S$: $0 + 0 = (+1) + (-1)$.

## Everyday Intuition

Strangeness is a label inherited from a particle's $s$-quark content. The strong force respects the label (it cannot create a single strange particle from nothing); the weak force can rewrite it by one unit at a time, which is slow and lets us actually see the strange particle before it decays.

## Why It Matters

Without strangeness conservation, the lifetimes of kaons and $\Lambda$ baryons would not make sense: produced in $10^{-23}\ \text{s}$ by the strong force, they live $10^{-10}\ \text{s}$ before decaying weakly. That huge ratio (~$10^{13}$) is direct experimental evidence for a separately conserved quantum number.

**Example — strong production:**

$$\pi^{-} + p \rightarrow K^{0} + \Lambda^{0}$$

$S$: $0 + 0 = 1 + (-1) = 0$. ✓ Allowed by the strong force.

**Example — weak decay of a strange particle:**

$$\Lambda^{0} \rightarrow p + \pi^{-}$$

$S$: $-1 \rightarrow 0$, so $\Delta S = +1$. ✓ Allowed by the weak interaction only.

## Common Mistakes

- Assigning $S = +1$ to the strange quark. The convention is $S(s) = -1$, $S(\bar{s}) = +1$.
- Allowing $\Delta S = \pm 2$ in a single weak decay. AQA restricts a single weak vertex to $\Delta S = 0$ or $\pm 1$.
- Forgetting strangeness when checking strong-interaction reactions.
- Saying strange particles are unstable because of the strong force. They are unstable because of the weak interaction.
