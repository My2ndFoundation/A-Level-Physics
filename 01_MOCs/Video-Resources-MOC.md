# Video-Resources-MOC

A curated, quality-first map of external YouTube explainers that make the more abstract Physics ideas click. Every video was hand-picked from reputable creators and each video ID was verified against YouTube (title + channel) before embedding.

Each entry links to its own page — with an embedded player, a short guide, and wikilinks into the concept pages it illuminates. Those concept pages carry a matching **## Watch** backlink.

> [!tip] How to use these
> Watch a video *alongside* the atomic pages it links to, not instead of them. The video builds intuition and interest; the wiki pages give the precise definitions, equations, assumptions and exam framing.

## Mechanics, Motion & Materials

- [[An-Introduction-to-Stress-and-Strain|An Introduction to Stress and Strain]] — The Efficient Engineer · ~15 min  
  A beautifully animated tour of stress, strain, the stress–strain curve, Hooke's law and Young's modulus, framed by real tensile testing. It makes the material-properties section of the course feel concrete.
- [[Conservation-of-Momentum-with-Dianna-Cowern|Conservation of Momentum - Physics 101 / AP Physics 1 Review with Dianna Cowern]] — Physics Girl · ~9 min  
  Dianna Cowern builds momentum conservation from real collision problems, so the algebra stays tied to physical pictures. Ideal for seeing why total momentum is conserved and how elastic vs inelastic cases differ.
- [[Newtons-Laws-of-Motion-and-Inertia|GCSE Physics - Newton's 1st & 2nd Laws  /  Circular Motion  /  Inertia (2026/27 exams)]] — Cognito · ~7 min  
  A clean, exam-aligned walk-through of inertia (first law) and F = ma (second law). A good confidence-building refresher before you meet the A-Level treatment, where the same laws are applied with vectors and resolved forces.
- [[The-Motion-of-Projectiles-Walter-Lewin-Lecture|Lec 04: The Motion of Projectiles  /  8.01 Classical Mechanics, Fall 1999 (Walter Lewin)]] — For the Allure of Physics (Walter Lewin) · ~53 min  
  Walter Lewin's legendary MIT lecture demonstrates, live, that horizontal and vertical motion are independent — the single idea that unlocks all projectile problems. Longer than a typical clip, but the demos are worth it.

## Circular Motion & Orbits

- [[All-of-Circular-Motion-A-Level|A Level Physics Revision: All of Circular Motion (in under 20 minutes!)]] — ZPhysics · ~18 min  
  A compact A-Level run-through of radians, period, angular velocity and centripetal acceleration/force (v²/r and ω²r) with worked examples. A strong single-video revision of the whole topic.
- [[Angular-Velocity-and-Speed|Angular velocity and speed  /  Uniform circular motion and gravitation  /  AP Physics 1  /  Khan Academy]] — Khan Academy · ~6 min  
  Introduces angular velocity measured in radians and derives the key link v = ωr between angular and linear speed — the bridge you need for every circular-motion calculation.
- [[Satellite-in-Circular-Orbit|Speed of a Satellite in Circular Orbit, Orbital Velocity, Period, Centripetal Force, Physics Problem]] — The Organic Chemistry Tutor · ~17 min  
  Shows gravity acting as the centripetal force that keeps a satellite in circular orbit, and derives orbital speed and period. A clean link between circular motion and gravitational fields.
- [[What-Is-a-Centripetal-Force|Physics - What Is a Centripetal Force?]] — The Organic Chemistry Tutor · ~6 min  
  Explains why circular motion always needs a centre-directed (centripetal) force, and clears up the persistent centripetal-vs-centrifugal confusion — the single most common trap in this topic.

## Electricity, Magnetism & Fields

- [[Electric-Fields-Crash-Course-Physics|Electric Fields: Crash Course Physics #26]] — CrashCourse · ~9 min  
  A brisk, well-animated introduction to Coulomb's law, field lines and field strength around point charges — a solid first pass before the algebra.
- [[Electromagnetic-Induction-Faradays-Law-Walter-Lewin|8.02x - Lect 16 - Electromagnetic Induction, Faraday's Law, Lenz Law, SUPER DEMO]] — Lectures by Walter Lewin · ~51 min  
  The classic MIT lecture that derives Faraday's and Lenz's laws from changing flux, punctuated by spectacular live demonstrations. The definitive deep-dive on induction.
- [[How-Special-Relativity-Makes-Magnets-Work|How Special Relativity Makes Magnets Work]] — Veritasium · ~7 min  
  Shows that magnetism is really electricity seen from a moving frame: length contraction turns a neutral wire into a charged one for a moving charge. A deep 'aha' for the force on charges and currents.
- [[How-a-DC-Electric-Motor-Works|How does an Electric Motor work? DC Motor explained]] — The Engineering Mindset · ~10 min  
  A clear animated walk-through of the force on a current-carrying coil in a magnetic field, and how the commutator turns that force into continuous rotation — the motor effect made visible.
- [[The-Biggest-Misconception-About-Electricity|The Biggest Misconception About Electricity]] — Veritasium · ~15 min  
  A genuinely mind-changing video: the energy in a circuit travels in the electromagnetic field around the wires, not carried along by slow-drifting electrons. It reframes what current and potential difference actually transport.
- [[What-Everyone-Gets-Wrong-About-Gravity|What Everyone Gets Wrong About Gravity]] — Veritasium · ~14 min  
  Explains gravitational fields, free fall and orbits through the curved-spacetime picture of general relativity. At A-Level gravity is a force (Newton), so treat the GR framing as enrichment — but it gives powerful intuition for why everything falls the same way.

## Oscillations & Waves

- [[A-Better-Description-of-Resonance|A better description of resonance]] — Steve Mould · ~9 min  
  Steve Mould gives an unusually clear picture of why systems respond strongly at their natural frequency, using a pendulum analogy and vivid demos. Great for forced oscillations, damping and resonance.
- [[Chladni-Figures-Standing-Waves-Made-Visible|Chladni Figures - random couscous snaps into beautiful patterns]] — Steve Mould · ~3 min  
  Couscous on a vibrating plate collects at the nodes, making two-dimensional standing-wave patterns appear from nowhere. A striking way to see nodes, antinodes and how frequency sets the mode.
- [[Pendulum-Waves-Harvard-Demonstration|Pendulum Waves]] — Harvard Natural Sciences Lecture Demonstrations · ~3 min  
  Fifteen pendulums of increasing length drift in and out of phase, creating mesmerising travelling- and standing-wave illusions. It shows viscerally how period depends on length and how phase differences accumulate.
- [[The-Doppler-Effect-Explained-Visually|The Doppler Effect explained visually]] — ScienceClic English · ~5 min  
  A clean animated visualisation of how relative motion between source and observer compresses or stretches wavelength, shifting the observed frequency. Exactly the mental model you want for Doppler questions.
- [[The-Original-Double-Slit-Experiment|The Original Double Slit Experiment]] — Veritasium · ~5 min  
  Veritasium recreates Young's experiment, showing how two coherent sources produce interference fringes. It is the bridge between classical wave interference and the wave–particle duality of light.
- [[What-Is-a-Fourier-Series-Superposition|But what is a Fourier series? From heat flow to drawing with circles]] — 3Blue1Brown · ~25 min  
  Shows visually that any complex wave is a sum of simple sinusoids of different amplitude, frequency and phase — the deep idea behind superposition. Enrichment-level maths, but it makes 'adding waves' click.

## Astrophysics

- [[Distances-and-Standard-Candles-Crash-Course|Distances: Crash Course Astronomy #25]] — CrashCourse · ~13 min  
  Builds the cosmic distance ladder from parallax up to brightness-based standard candles — the chain of reasoning that lets us put numbers on astronomical distances and underpins Hubble's law.
- [[How-We-Detect-Exoplanets-Crash-Course|Exoplanets: Crash Course Astronomy #27]] — CrashCourse · ~12 min  
  Explains the two workhorse methods for finding planets around other stars — the transit dip in brightness and the radial-velocity (Doppler) wobble. A satisfying application of light and Doppler ideas.
- [[Life-Cycle-of-Stars-Crash-Course|Low Mass Stars: Crash Course Astronomy #29]] — CrashCourse · ~13 min  
  Traces a Sun-like star's whole life — main-sequence hydrogen fusion, red-giant phase, and white-dwarf end state. The narrative that makes stellar evolution memorable rather than a list of stages.
- [[Light-and-Black-Body-Radiation-Crash-Course|Light: Crash Course Astronomy #24]] — CrashCourse · ~11 min  
  Explains how a hot object's colour and spectrum depend on its temperature — the black-body behaviour behind Wien's displacement law and Stefan's law, and how we read a star's temperature from its colour.
- [[Stars-and-the-HR-Diagram-Crash-Course|Stars: Crash Course Astronomy #26]] — CrashCourse · ~11 min  
  Introduces the OBAFGKM spectral classes and plots stars on the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram (luminosity vs temperature). The HR diagram is central to A-Level astrophysics, and this makes it click.

  Phil Plait explains how refracting and reflecting telescopes gather light and why a larger aperture gives greater resolving power. Sets up the whole 'how we observe the universe' strand.
